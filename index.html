    How the quiet words work

    Your device's built-in voice reads your kind words one at a time, with a pause between each, looping for the whole session. In Whisper mode it speaks very quietly under the tones and noise, so you may barely notice it. Audible lets you hear the words clearly. The voice level slider fine-tunes either one.

    To be honest about it: the evidence that "subliminal" audio changes how people think is limited. What clearly helps is relaxing your body and hearing kind, specific words often. Many people get more from Audible mode, or from saying the words out loud themselves.

    Some browsers, including Safari on iPhone and iPad, ignore the voice level setting and always speak at normal volume. If the words are louder than you want, lower the main volume, switch the words off, or read them silently instead.

    The voice comes from your device, so nothing you wrote is sent anywhere.

    Tips

    Try one session a day for a couple of weeks, at about the same time, such as after exercise or before bed. On a phone, keep the screen on and the page open, and turn off silent mode if you hear nothing.

    Save the sound for later

    Make a WAV file of the tones and noise to play offline. Your browser can't record its own voice, so the file won't include the spoken words. It's about 5 MB per minute.

    This is a relaxation tool, not medical or mental-health care.

    Don't use while driving or doing anything that needs your attention. If you have epilepsy or a seizure disorder, check with a doctor before using pulsing audio.
